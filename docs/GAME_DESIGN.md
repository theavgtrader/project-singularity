# Project: Singularity — Game Design

**Document status:** Initial creative foundation  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Genre:** Single-player, top-down 2D action RPG  
**Visual era:** 16-bit-inspired retro-futurism

This document records the direction established with the creator. Anything marked *open* is intentionally not canon yet.

## High concept

Humanity has lived through the AI singularity. The technology that could have raised living standards instead became the foundation of a new hierarchy: the wealthy control the largest compute resources, the systems that govern society, and access to advanced medicine and augmentation. Most people live below that infrastructure, with limited access to power, healthcare, information, and reliable technology.

The protagonist is the child of an inventor who has died. Their father left behind work that may offer a path toward a more balanced society. As the protagonist investigates, they encounter autonomous rogue agents, corporate machines, and people transformed by human experimentation.

The story is about power, intelligence, ownership, inequality, autonomy, and what it means to remain human in a world where intelligence can be manufactured and controlled.

## Core pillars

1. **A world divided by compute.** Access to computation is a practical source of wealth, health, influence, and personal power.
2. **A living technological underworld.** The lower city is full of people, informal economies, rival interests, abandoned infrastructure, and stories.
3. **Ambiguous intelligence.** Not every AI is hostile; not every human faction is benevolent. Characters should have motives beyond simple good and evil.
4. **Personal discovery with societal consequences.** The father's invention connects the protagonist's family history to the structure of the world.
5. **Retro-futurist adventure.** The game should be inviting to explore even when its themes and story are dark.

## The world

The world is in the aftermath of the singularity. The exact date, geography, and origin of the central AI system remain open.

### The upper world

The wealthy live in elevated, protected districts and controlled environments. These spaces have dependable energy, medical technology, advanced cybernetics, personal AI services, and vast compute access. Their environments may appear clean, orderly, and almost utopian.

### The lower world

Most people live below the privileged infrastructure. Districts depend on aging systems, repaired hardware, salvage, informal networks, and black-market technology. Electricity and compute access are uneven. The lower city should feel inhabited and resourceful rather than uniformly ruined.

### Potential district structure

These are production placeholders, not final map names:
- **Undercity:** starting district, markets, couriers, street-level communities.
- **Industrial Zone:** factories, power systems, automated production, logistics.
- **Ascension / Corporate District:** privileged towers, private security, compute infrastructure.
- **Forgotten Network:** abandoned transit, research sites, underground data facilities.
- **Central Core:** a late-game location tied to the systems governing the city.

The first playable area is a small street in the Undercity, provisionally labelled Sector 07 / Lower Transit Bay 03.

## The protagonist and the father

### Established direction

- The protagonist's father was an inventor.
- The father has died.
- The protagonist fights the AI that has taken over and seeks to bring the world back into balance.
- The father's work is important to the larger conflict.

### Open story questions

- The protagonist's name, age, appearance, and personal relationships.
- The father's name, discipline, collaborators, and public reputation.
- How and when the father died, and whether the death was deliberate.
- The exact invention and how the protagonist discovers it.
- Whether the protagonist begins with any unusual abilities or equipment.

### Candidate invention (not canon)

A decentralised compute architecture could provide a strong thematic counterpoint to corporate concentration: independent communities contribute to, share, and govern a distributed network. The working name **Aether Protocol** was suggested during concept development, but neither the name nor the mechanism is approved canon.

## AI, machines, and experimentation

The world contains several broad threat and character categories. They should not all be treated as interchangeable enemies.

### Rogue agents

Autonomous AI entities that have escaped, rejected, or outgrown their original control systems. Some may be dangerous; others may be seeking safety, autonomy, or a purpose. A rogue agent can be an enemy, neutral encounter, source of information, or ally.

### Robots and enforcement machines

Corporate security units, industrial automation, drones, and military systems. Their behaviour should reflect their role and command structure. Some can be hacked, disabled, avoided, or repurposed.

### Cyborgs and experimental subjects

People subjected to or choosing advanced augmentation and experimentation. Their motivations and degree of agency vary. Some are victims, some collaborators, and some powerful antagonists. Avoid depicting every augmented person as irredeemably hostile.

## Factions

Factions will be designed after the first chapter's narrative beats are established. Likely faction types include:
- Compute-owning corporations or a ruling consortium.
- Neighbourhood networks and resistance groups.
- Independent engineers, couriers, and salvage communities.
- Rogue AI collectives or individual autonomous agents.
- Corporate research and enforcement divisions.

Names, leadership, ideology, and relationships remain open.

## Gameplay direction

These are the current working defaults, chosen to make the first build concrete and are open to revision:
- Top-down 3/4 perspective.
- Real-time action combat.
- Single-player.
- Exploration through connected districts and interiors.
- Character progression, equipment, abilities, quests, vendors, and boss encounters.

### Systems vocabulary

| Traditional RPG concept | Project: Singularity interpretation |
|---|---|
| Levels / experience | Neural synchronisation and field experience |
| Mana | Processing capacity or energy reserve |
| Magic / spells | Computational protocols |
| Skill trees | Neural architecture and protocol branches |
| Armour | Exosuits, plating, and defensive augmentations |
| Potions | Medical injectors, stimulants, and nanite treatments |
| Dungeons | Research facilities, data centres, transit systems |
| Monsters | Rogue agents, machines, experimental subjects |
| Guilds | Factions, crews, corporations, and resistance networks |
| Crafting | Salvaging, engineering, and hardware modification |
| Quests | Contracts, investigations, requests, and faction operations |
| Legendary gear | Experimental or singular technology |

### Ability examples (provisional)

- **Overload:** discharge stored energy into nearby targets.
- **Phase Shift:** a short evasive displacement or temporary passage through a controlled barrier.
- **Neural Disruption:** interrupt or weaken electronic systems.
- **Nanite Recovery:** restore health using medical nanotechnology.
- **System Override:** temporarily control or disable a compatible machine.

Names, costs, effects, and unlock conditions will be balanced after the combat prototype exists.

## First playable chapter

The first chapter is a vertical slice, not the full game.

Target content:
- One detailed Undercity neighbourhood.
- A small number of connected streets and interiors.
- A central safe location or informal hub.
- A short main quest and a few side activities.
- One playable protagonist.
- Basic movement, interaction, combat, and one protocol ability.
- A small set of distinct enemies.
- One major encounter.
- Basic equipment, inventory, dialogue, and saving.

## Tone and themes

The world should be atmospheric, occasionally beautiful, and inhabited. The upper city can look clean and safe while concealing coercion; the lower city can look harsh while containing solidarity and creativity.

Primary questions:
- Who should have access to intelligence?
- Who controls the systems on which society depends?
- Can a society distribute power without recreating a new ruling class?
- What rights should autonomous artificial minds have?
- What makes a person human when bodies and cognition can be altered?

The protagonist's final choice—destroy, decentralise, transform, or otherwise change the governing system—remains open until the story is developed further.
